# CosmoVision — Science & Data Notes

This document explains how CosmoVision maps real astronomy onto a compressible,
 explorable 3D scene, and where the line is drawn between measured data and
illustration.

## Two scale regimes

| Regime | 1 scene unit means | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Galaxy | ≈ 4,166.7 light-years (12 units ≈ 100,000 ly disc) | Milky Way, nebula, starfield |
| System | 1 AU | Sun, planets, moons, belts |

The Solar System is anchored at `(8.6, 3.2, 2.2)` inside the galaxy disc — lifted
above the mid-plane so system orbits never intersect the star particles. At
galaxy scale the system collapses into a selectable marker; once the camera
closer than ~22 units from the anchor, the galaxy layers fade out so they never
clutter close-up work.

## Orbital motion (compressed, not N-body)

Positions are computed from a deterministic schedule, not gravity:

```
angle(simDays) = phase(id) + 2π · simDays / visualPeriodDays
visualPeriodDays = compressPeriod(realPeriodDays)
compressPeriod(d) = 365.25 · (d / 365.25)^0.55
```

The 0.55 exponent compresses Neptune's 164.8-year orbit into a few thousand
simulated days, so all eight planets are watchable in one sitting while
**preserving order and relative rhythm** (Mercury still laps Neptune many times).
Orbits are circles in each body's own plane; inclination tilts the plane,
eccentricity styles the orbit line. Axial spin uses `visualSpinRate` (radians per
simulated day) — animation only.

Simulation time advances as `days += dt · speed` with `dt` clamped to 50 ms, so a
background tab never teleports the sky. Speed presets are 0.1 / 1 / 10 / 100 /
1000 simulated days per real second.

## Data sources & precision

Every number in the info panel is a `Fact` with a precision label:

| Precision | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `measured` | Instrument/lab value with accepted uncertainty | Mars mean diameter 6,792 km |
| `approximate` | Rounded or derived value | Kuiper Belt extent ≈ 30–50 AU |
| `illustrative` | Chosen for the visualization | Scene radius in units |
| `procedural` | Generated by the app, not observed | Texture patterns, galaxy arms |

Sources: NASA Planetary Fact Sheets, NASA Solar System Exploration, NASA/STScI
Hubble heritage, and the IAU (planet definitions, solar system body counts).
Source attribution appears on every fact when **Science mode** is enabled.

## What is procedural (illustration)

- Planet surfaces: 2D canvas textures seeded from colour palettes (banded gas
  giants, cratered rock, polar caps for Earth/Mars). Not photographs, not AI.
- The Milky Way: ~42,000 GPU particles in a seeded four-arm spiral with Gaussian
  scatter, radial colour ramps and differential rotation resolved in the vertex
  shader.
- Asteroid belts: instanced dodecahedra with a seeded LCG; Kuiper objects are
  icy-blue, main-belt objects rocky-grey.
- Starfield: a static spherical shell of ~5,000 stars with a blackbody-ish colour
  ramp, plus a few hundred bright glints.

## What is source-backed

- Presence, names, categories and parent relationships of all 17 objects.
- All facts in the info panel (diameters, masses, atmospheres, temperatures,
  periods, rotation rates, axial tilts, moon counts).
- Real semi-major axes and real orbital periods (`orbit.real*` fields), shown in
  the comparison table and science mode.

## Honest limitations

- No N-body gravity, relativity, or ephemeris precision — positions are
  illustrative at any given timestamp.
- Moon orbits are simplified circles around the (also moving) parent planet.
- Light-time delay, aberration and occlusion are not simulated.
- The galaxy's arm count/spread are artistic parameters inspired by observed
  spirals, not fitted to Milky Way surveys.
